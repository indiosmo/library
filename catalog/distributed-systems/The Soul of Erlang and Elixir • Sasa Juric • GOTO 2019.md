ABSTRACT
Whether you're developing a small web site or a large-scale distributed system, Erlang and Elixir are great tools for building server-side systems. Both languages can help you start quickly and deal with highly complex problems at a large scale.
This talk aims to explain what makes Erlang and Elixir suitable for such tasks. We'll look past the syntax and the ecosystem, focusing instead on the concurrency model of these languages.
Combining a bit of high-level theory and a couple of demos, we'll examine some important properties of Erlang/Elixir concurrency, with a special emphasis on why these properties are very relevant when building highly available systems.

Saša Jurić is a Croatian developer, longtime Elixir mentor, author of Manning's "Elixir in Action," and writer of The Erlangelist blog, who came to the BEAM languages after building a highly concurrent system in Erlang to escape the limits of Ruby on Rails. The talk dwells on the BEAM (Bogdan/Bjorn's Erlang Abstract Machine) concurrency model: millions of cheap, isolated, share-nothing processes communicating by asynchronous message passing and scheduled across CPU cores, which removes the need for locks, mutexes, and semaphores. Its opening chapter pays tribute to Joe Armstrong, Erlang's co-creator, who had died in April 2019 only months before this GOTO Chicago talk.
