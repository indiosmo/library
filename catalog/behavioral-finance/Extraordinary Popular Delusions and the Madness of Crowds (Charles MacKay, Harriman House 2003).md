## Auto Summary

Charles Mackay's Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds is a classic study of crowd psychology and mass delusion, first published in 1841 as Memoirs of Extraordinary Popular Delusions and revised in 1852. The cataloged Harriman House edition appeared in 2003 and highlights Mackay's accounts of financial manias such as John Law's Mississippi Scheme, the South Sea Bubble, and Dutch Tulipomania. Mackay argues that people often succumb collectively to speculative greed, superstition, fashion, and fear, then recover judgment only slowly and individually.
