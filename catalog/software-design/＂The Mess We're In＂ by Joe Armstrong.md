## Description

Joe Armstrong is one of the inventors of Erlang. When at the Ericsson computer science lab in 1986, he was part of the team who designed and implemented the first version of Erlang. He has written several Erlang books including Programming Erlang Software for a Concurrent World. Joe has a PhD in computer science from the Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm, Sweden.

## Auto Summary

Delivered as the opening keynote at Strange Loop 2014, this is one of the most beloved talks by Joe Armstrong (1950-2019), who co-created Erlang at Ericsson's computer science lab in 1986 and died in 2019. Its central provocation is that although hardware has grown thousands of times faster, software keeps getting worse: a 1985 machine booted in about 60 seconds, so a machine thousands of times faster ought to boot in milliseconds. Armstrong argues for choosing clarity over efficiency (wait a decade and the hardware will be a thousand times faster anyway), laments the loss of a common lingua franca that shell, make, and C once provided, and ties software's unmanageable state-space to the physical limits of computation.
