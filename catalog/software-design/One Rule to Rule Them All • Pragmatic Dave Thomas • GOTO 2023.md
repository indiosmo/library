ABSTRACT
As developers, we live under a constant barrage of helpful advice, often in the form of rules with cute names. We're told how to design, how to code, how to deploy, how to monitor: there are systems and rules for everything. How can we keep up? How can we know what's good advice, and what's bad?

Over time I've come to realize that just about every good piece of advice is actually a special case of something both more general and simpler. So, let me introduce you to the "one rule to rule them all." [...]

## Auto Summary

Dave Thomas ("PragDave") is co-author with Andy Hunt of The Pragmatic Programmer (1999), one of the seventeen original signatories of the 2001 Agile Manifesto, founder of the Pragmatic Bookshelf publishing house, and the person who introduced Ruby to much of the English-speaking world through the 2000 "PickAxe" book Programming Ruby. The "one rule" framing here restates ETC ("Easier To Change"), the guiding principle he and Hunt placed at the center of the 20th-anniversary edition of The Pragmatic Programmer, and the talk extends the argument of his widely circulated 2015 "Agile is Dead (Long Live Agility)" keynote, which criticized the commercialization of capital-A Agile. His emphasis on intuition, paper, and the conscious/subconscious mind draws on Kahneman's Thinking, Fast and Slow and Gallwey's The Inner Game of Tennis, both cited in the talk.
