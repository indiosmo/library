## Description

"The Mother of All Demos is a name given retrospectively to Douglas Engelbart's December 9, 1968, demonstration of experimental computer technologies that are now commonplace. The live demonstration featured the introduction of the computer mouse, video conferencing, teleconferencing, hypertext, word processing, hypermedia, object addressing and dynamic file linking, bootstrapping, and a collaborative real-time editor."

## Auto Summary

Douglas Engelbart (1925-2013) led the Augmentation Research Center at Stanford Research Institute (SRI), where his team built the oN-Line System (NLS) shown here; the 90-minute presentation was delivered to about 1,000 attendees at the Fall Joint Computer Conference in San Francisco, with a leased microwave video link and homebuilt modems connecting Engelbart on stage to the SRI computer 30 miles away in Menlo Park. The work was framed by his 1962 manifesto "Augmenting Human Intellect" and funded by ARPA and NASA; the mouse on display was built by Bill English from Engelbart's design. The staging and computer-supported teleconferencing were orchestrated by Stewart Brand, and the system's ideas went on to shape Xerox PARC and, through it, the Macintosh and modern personal computing.
