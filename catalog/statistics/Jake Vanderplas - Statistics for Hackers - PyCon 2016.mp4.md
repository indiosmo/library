## Description

Speaker: Jake Vanderplas

Statistics has the reputation of being difficult to understand, but using some simple Python skills it can be made much more intuitive. This talk will cover several sampling-based approaches to solving statistical problems, and show you that if you can write a for-loop, you can do statistics.

Slides can be found at: https://speakerdeck.com/pycon2016 and https://github.com/PyCon/2016-slides

Jake VanderPlas was, at the time, a data scientist at the University of Washington eScience Institute, trained as an astronomer and co-author of the award-winning Statistics, Data Mining, and Machine Learning in Astronomy; he later moved to Google and wrote the Python Data Science Handbook. The talk organizes its computational approach into four recipes for hacking statistics: direct simulation, shuffling, bootstrapping, and cross-validation, deliberately favoring intuitive sampling methods over memorized formulas. Its Dr. Seuss-themed examples (the star-bellied Sneetches and Yertle's turtle tower) illustrate hypothesis testing and resampling, and VanderPlas closes by flagging two topics he skips: Bayesian methods (pointing to Cameron Davidson-Pilon's Bayesian Methods for Hackers) and selection bias.
