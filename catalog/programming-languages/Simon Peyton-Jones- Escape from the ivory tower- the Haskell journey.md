Simon Peyton Jones is a British computer scientist who spent decades at Microsoft Research Cambridge (later moving to Epic Games) and is the principal architect of the Glasgow Haskell Compiler (GHC), the dominant Haskell implementation. Delivered as Churchill College Cambridge's annual Computer Science lecture, the talk recounts how Haskell grew from a 1987 committee effort to unify competing lazy functional languages, and credits collaborators like Philip Wadler, whose work on monads and type classes (a Wadler-Blott invention) gave the language its principled approach to I/O and overloading. He frames the language's longevity through his well-known motto 'avoid success at all costs' -- staying free to redesign by not chasing mainstream adoption too early -- and reflects on deep simple principles such as 'laziness keeps you pure.'
